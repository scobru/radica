How Radica works

Radica has no company and no central moderators. Everything is signed by its author and stored on Freenet, and what you see is decided on your own device: by who you follow, whose blocklists you trust, and a few checks that run on your computer.

🔑 Your identity

You are a key, not an account: no email, no phone number, no password. Either Radica makes a key on this device, or you sign in with Viso, your Freenet identity for all its apps.

Back up your key (Identity page). Nobody can reset it for you: lose it and that identity is gone.

👀 Who sees what

Public: your profile, posts, comments, likes, who you follow, and communities. Anyone on Freenet can read them.

Private: direct messages, private groups and Broadcast Channels (one-to-many channels where admins post and subscribers read, optionally linked to a discussion group) are end-to-end encrypted: only the people in them can read them. A Secret Chat uses per-message forward-secrecy keys, stays strictly on this device (never synced across devices), and supports disappearing message timers.

River contacts (marked "River") are people using River, Freenet's chat app. You can message them, but River's direct messages carry text only: no reactions, reply quotes or edits.

🌱 Linfa and reposts

Linfa is the sap of the plant on every profile. Your node counts it from the posts, comments and likes it holds: nothing is stored, so nobody can write themselves a number.

  • Answering counts most: a comment on someone's post 3, being reposted 3, a comment received 2, a like received 1, a like given ½. One answer per person and post.
  • Posting grows the first leaves only: one for each day you post, thirty at most. The rest comes from other people.
  • It flows through roots: the people you follow count whole, their friends half, strangers a quarter and only a little in all. So two readers can see two different plants on the same profile.
  • Ages: seed, seedling, sapling, young tree, tree, elder tree. Lighter leaves are what grew in the last week.

Repost (under a post) shares it on your feed, with your own words if you add any. The author is told, and your reposts have their own tab on your profile.

🌿 Trust badges

Badges are worked out on your device from who you follow, so two people can see different badges on the same person.

  • 🌿 Rooted: you follow them, or they are a curator you trust. Most of the people you see are, so next to posts it is left out; their profile still shows it.
  • 🌱 Trusted: someone you follow follows them (a friend of a friend).
  • ✨ Supporter: a verified donor to Radica on the public supporters wall. Counts as trusted, and shows even on people you follow.
  • 👻 GhostKey: their identity is vouched for by a Freenet GhostKey (an anonymous key Freenet gives its donors). Counts as trusted, like a supporter.
  • ✨ New: outside your network. Nothing wrong with that: follow people to grow it.

A green mod next to a name means they moderate the community the post is in.

👎 Dislikes and shielded posts

Only reactions from your own network count: Rooted people and community moderators weigh 3 for a dislike and 2 for a like; Trusted people (friends of friends, supporters and GhostKey holders) weigh 1.5 for a dislike and 1 for a like; everyone else 0.

Posts carry a small anti-bot proof: a second or two of work your device does when you publish (not needed once you are on the supporters wall or have a GhostKey). A post by someone outside your network without it was likely written by a script around Radica, so it is shielded too. Comments carry a smaller one, made in the background; a comment by someone outside your network without it waits behind a show button.

When trusted dislikes outweigh trusted likes, the post is shielded: covered, with the reason, and one click shows it anyway. Turn this off in Blocklists → Web-of-Trust Moderation.

🚫 Hide, block and report
  • Hide user (on their profile, or in a post's ⋯ menu): you stop seeing them. Private: only you know.
  • Block: goes on your public blocklist. You stop seeing them and chatting with them, and so does everyone who trusts your blocklist. In River groups their messages are hidden for you too; banning them from the room itself is up to its owner and moderators.
  • Report a post (in its ⋯ menu): also on your public blocklist, with your reason. The post is hidden for you and for whoever trusts your list, and so is its picture or video wherever it is shared again; Radica's maintainer and moderators are notified (and in a community, its creator and moderators too).

In Blocklists you choose whose lists to follow. Radica's maintainer's list is followed by default; you can stop following it at any time.

Activity shows first what comes from your network. Notifications from strangers wait under Others and do not count as unread, so nobody can fill your bell; one that points to a real comment on your post counts as normal.

Radica's moderators, named by the maintainer, keep the public timeline clean in an emergency: their 🛡️ Remove on a post or a comment hides it for everyone who follows the maintainer's list. They show under Blocklists; stop following the maintainer's list and their removals no longer apply to you. Communities keep their own moderators and rules.

🏛️ Communities and moderators

A community's creator and its moderators can remove a post from that community and ban people from it. They cannot touch your profile or your own feed: a removed post still exists there.

An offensive community is up to its own moderators, and, when they do nothing, to Radica's moderators (named by the maintainer): they can hide a community (the 🛡️ button in its header, or Moderation → Hidden communities) for everyone who follows Radica's moderation, and ban whoever runs it. A hidden community leaves their lists, their search and their timelines, and so do its posts; what the people banned by Radica write in a community's chat is hidden too. Nothing is deleted from the network: someone who stops following the maintainer's list sees it all again. r/radica cannot be hidden.

🌐 Translation

Posts, comments and messages in another language show Translate: the text is translated on your computer, into the language you pick on the Identity page. Nothing leaves your device; the first time downloads the language model (about 22 MB).

You can also translate what you write before publishing it: the 🌐 menu next to the post, comment and chat boxes. Links, @mentions, #tags and code stay as you wrote them, and ↩ Undo brings your words back.

🛡️ Checks on your device
  • Images and videos are scanned on your computer before you post (for videos, a frame every 2 seconds). Clearly explicit: posting is blocked. Possibly sensitive: posted blurred until clicked, and you can untick that if the scanner is wrong. If the scanner can't run, media can't be posted.
  • Personal data: a phone number, email, card number, IBAN or tax ID in what you write asks before it is published. You choose: publish as written, or go back and edit.
  • Offensive words: what you write is published as you wrote it. Each reader chooses whether offensive words show as asterisks (Identity → Offensive Words, on by default).
  • Limits: a short pause between posts, at most 10 posts a minute, and no duplicate of your last 5 posts.
🧱 Against spam and bots: everything in one place

Radica has no server and anyone can make a key for free, so nothing here can stop a determined attacker. The aim is to make spam costly, keep it out of your way, and give people who clean up the tools to do it. Four layers:

1. On your device: only the people who use Radica meet these; a script that writes straight to the network skips them.

  • A pause between posts (15 seconds for newcomers, 2 once vouched by someone you trust, a supporter receipt or a GhostKey), 1 second between comments, at most 10 posts a minute, no duplicate of your last 5 posts.
  • The scan of images and videos, and the check for personal data (see Checks on your device).

2. Checked by every reader: these cannot be skipped, only paid for.

  • Proof of work: a second or two of calculation on every post (a fraction of a second on a comment) and a few seconds, once per session, when you announce yourself. A stranger's post without it is covered, a comment without it waits behind a show button. Supporters on the wall and GhostKey holders do not need it.
  • Dates in the future: a post dated more than 5 minutes ahead is not shown (it would stay on top for good).

3. Your own network decides: so two people can see different things.

  • Likes and dislikes weigh by who gives them (roots, friends of friends, strangers 0): see Trust badges and Dislikes and shielded posts. Top uses the same weights.
  • Explore always asks for the people you follow and up to 40 of your friends of friends, supporters and GhostKeys, plus a fixed lane of newcomers (30 people) drawn by the work in their announcement: a flood of fake accounts can fill that lane and nothing else.
  • Activity: notifications from strangers wait under Others, unless a real comment on your post is behind them.
  • Communities can be open only to vouched members.

4. People who clean up: Hide (private), Block and Report (a public blocklist you can share), the blocklists you choose to follow, community moderators, and Radica's moderators, who have a Moderation page (reported posts, bans, and hidden communities) with the posts people reported, the ones reported by people they trust first (a report from a fresh key weighs nothing, so a swarm of fake accounts cannot push a post up the queue; theirs wait in a fold), and can remove, ban or dismiss: what they remove or ban is hidden for everyone following the maintainer's list.

What a newcomer meets: a 15-second pause between posts and a few seconds of proof; the posts show in Recent from the first one, but not in Top or Following until someone in the reader's network likes or follows them; their comments show, with the proof. A newcomer on an old version of the app, without the proof, is covered until they update. A supporter badge, a GhostKey, or being followed by someone trusted lifts these limits.

What it does not stop: someone with a lot of computing power can still make many accounts with valid proofs. They then fill the newcomers' lane and are covered or hidden for people outside their reach, and reports and blocklists do the rest. Each layer is a brake, none is a wall.

✨ Supporter badge

Donate USDC on Base from the donate page (Identity → Support). The money goes straight to the project through a contract nobody can change. You get a zero-knowledge proof: paste it here, an attester checks it on-chain, and your badge appears within minutes. It shows your tier, never which wallet paid. Showing it on the public supporters wall is your choice.

What it gives you, with the same perks at every tier (🌱 Sprout $1+, 🌿 Root $50+, 🌳 Oak $250+):

  • No newcomer limits: you post without the anti-bot proof of work (once you are on the public wall), with a 2-second pause between posts instead of 15, and you can start threads in communities open only to vouched members.
  • Trusted by everyone, if you are on the public supporters wall: every reader counts you as Trusted, so your posts show in their Following tab and your dislikes weigh 1.5 instead of 0.
  • The ✨ Supporter badge on your posts and profile.
  • A voice in the 🌱 Greenhouse: here the tier counts, see below.

Off the wall, nobody else can tell you donated: the perks on your own device still apply, the badge and the trust for others do not.

Already have a Freenet GhostKey? Identity → Support → 👻 Verify with GhostKey: your GhostKey (kept by your Freenet node) signs a proof that it vouches for this identity, or the proof you already made in Viso is reused. You get the same perks as a supporter, with the 👻 GhostKey badge, and your Greenhouse vote weighs like 🌳 Oak. The proof is public and shows which GhostKey it is, never who you are; Remove takes it back.

🌱 Greenhouse

Where supporters and GhostKey holders suggest what Radica should do next. A supporter or GhostKey holder plants a seed: a 💡 feature or a 🐛 bug, open for 3 to 30 days. Everyone eligible votes until it closes; then it shows 🌱 Sprouted (more for than against) or 🥀 Withered.

  • Votes weigh by tier: 🌱 Sprout 1, 🌿 Root 2, 🌳 Oak and 👻 GhostKey 3. You can change your vote, or take it back by pressing it again.
  • A bug takes only ▲ ("me too"): a bug is there or it is not.
  • Each seed and vote carries the supporter's receipt, so every reader checks it on their own device: your tier is visible to others.
  • The maintainer marks what became of a seed: Considering, Planned, Done or Not now.

Anyone can read the Greenhouse. The votes show what the community thinks: they guide, they do not decide.

✏️ Editing and deleting

Edit: your post shows the new text and an "edited" mark. Apps that are not updated yet still show the original.

Delete: the post is removed from the network and never comes back. But on a network with no owner, anyone who already saw it may have kept a copy: post as if it could last.
